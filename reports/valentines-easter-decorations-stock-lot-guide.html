Valentine's Day & Easter Stock Lots — the B2B Seasonal Buying Guide

What China's second-tier holiday surplus actually looks like on the clearance market — where it comes from (post-holiday retailer residue, cancelled spring exports, gift-chain tails), indicative price bands for Valentine plush, gift boxes, Easter egg décor and baskets, the two-peak seasonal clock with early-buy vs counter-season trade-offs, LED battery and children's-egg-kit compliance lines, IP notes, and a five-step pre-payment checklist.

Key takeaways.

  • Two peaks, two windows: Valentine surplus clears late February–March and Easter surplus clears April–May; early-buy supply (fresh production for the coming peak) appears November–January for Valentine and January–March for Easter.
  • Indicative bands: Valentine plush typically USD 0.5–2.5/pc, gift boxes and wrap USD 0.05–0.5/pc, light-up props USD 0.5–3/pc, Easter egg décor and kits USD 0.05–0.4/pc, baskets USD 0.5–2/pc, garden flags USD 0.8–3/pc.
  • Missed sailing windows strand goods for a full year: spring holidays have hard ship-by dates — cancelled export containers that miss the window price aggressively because the seller faces a 12-month hold.
  • IP risk is lower than Christmas or Halloween: generic hearts, bunnies and eggs are the safe core; skip franchise-character prints without licensing documents and audit gift-box prints for borrowed brand marks.
  • Compliance lines: LED props follow the battery line (UN38.3/MSDS), children's egg-painting kits follow toy-safety lines (EN71/ASTM-style), scented candles need SDS, and chocolate or filled confectionery is the no-go line.

1. Why the second-tier holidays behave differently

Christmas is one giant peak; Valentine's Day and Easter are two smaller peaks with harder edges. Goods must be on shelves by mid-February (Valentine) and 4–6 weeks before Easter Sunday (which moves between late March and late April each year). That timing pressure shapes the surplus market in three ways:

  • Hard ship-by dates. A container that misses its sailing window by two weeks is worthless for the current season — the seller either dumps it immediately or holds it a full year. Missed-window cancelled orders are therefore the deepest-priced lots in this category.
  • Short residue windows. Retailers dump unsold Valentine stock almost immediately after February 14 and Easter stock right after the holiday — the surplus is fresh, clean and concentrated into a few weeks, unlike the long Christmas tail.
  • Gift-adjacent supply. Valentine draws surplus from gift wrap, ribbons, favour boxes and jewelry-chain packaging lines; Easter draws from craft kits, egg décor and garden-flag lines — sources that pure décor buyers often overlook.

2. Where the surplus comes from

  • Post-holiday retailer residue. The signature door: unsold seasonal stock clears within weeks after the holiday — fresh goods, intact ranges, aggressive pricing.
  • Cancelled export orders. Finished spring containers that lose their buyer or miss the sailing window — the missed-window case prices hardest of all.
  • Gift and jewelry-chain tails. Valentine gift boxes, ribbons, favour bags and jewelry packaging overruns — packaging-grade stock that resells to gift and hamper businesses year-round.
  • Factory overproduction. Egg décor, baskets and bunny plush produced ahead of forecast; when the season's demand disappoints, the tail clears after the holiday.
  • Packaging and colour refreshes. Redesigns strand finished goods in old packaging — the product is unchanged and typically sells at a packaging discount.

4. The two-peak clock: early-buy vs counter-season

This category runs a calendar with two usable windows per holiday — the same clock logic as the seasonal buying calendar, compressed into tighter peaks:

Practical read: buyers with dry storage and patient capital win with counter-season residue; buyers reselling to retail in-season win with early-buy lots priced just below wholesale. The hold-cost math — storage rent, capital tied up, and plush/paper aging — decides which window pays better for you, not a universal rule.

5. Compliance lines: batteries, craft kits, candles, food

  • LED light-up props. Neon hearts, light-up frames and LED candle sets with lithium cells follow the battery shipping line — UN38.3 test summaries, MSDS and watt-hour documentation. Confirm the battery paperwork matches the production batch before committing; the same line applies as in the Christmas decorations guide.
  • Children's craft and egg kits. Egg-painting sets, egg-hunt kits and children's craft bundles are children's products in most destination markets — EN71-style toy-safety compliance in the EU, ASTM F963-style in the US. Undocumented kits limit resale channels; documented kits command a premium.
  • Scented candles and fragrance goods. Filled candles and diffusers follow the filled-goods line: SDS per formula and destination-market hazard labelling (CLP-style in the EU), with readable date codes.
  • Food — the no-go. Chocolate, filled eggs and candy sets cannot ship as ordinary clearance cargo (food import regimes, temperature, shelf life). Lots that include food lines should be split — take the non-food goods only.

6. Aging and grading checks

  • Plush. The volume SKU of Valentine lots. Smell-test for damp-storage odour, squeeze-test for matted pile, and check stitched eyes and seams. Bottom-layer cartons tell the truth; counter-season lots held 10+ months need the strictest plush screening.
  • Paper goods. Gift boxes, wrap and décor yellow and scuff in storage — open cartons from the middle and bottom of the stack, not just the top. Crushed boxes limit retail-channel resale but still clear to gift-trade and market stalls.
  • Prints and packaging variants. Seasonal prints date themselves (year prints, dated ribbons); confirm no year marking where resell windows are long, and audit prints for borrowed brand or character marks.
  • Set completeness. Egg-hunt kits, craft bundles and gift sets strand incomplete in storage — count against the packing list per carton sample, the same set-completeness trap as toy and kitchen lots.

7. How to buy: five-step checklist

Step 1 — Pick your window honestly

Counter-season residue only pays if you can hold 10–12 months at real storage cost; otherwise target early-buy lots or missed-window dumps that resell inside the season.

Step 2 — Demand three proofs

  • full carton photos including bottom layers;
  • a live warehouse video with a handwritten date card, opening one carton from the middle of the stack;
  • UN38.3/MSDS for LED lines, toy-safety documents for children's kits, SDS for scented goods.

Step 3 — Hand-test the aging points

Smell and squeeze plush, open middle-layer paper cartons, check print scuffing, and look for year markings on prints and ribbons.

Step 4 — Verify completeness and the no-go line

Count kit and gift-set contents per carton sample, and confirm the lot contains no chocolate, filled eggs or other food lines — split those out entirely.

Step 5 — Audit the prints

Generic hearts and bunnies are the safe core; any recognizable character or brand print without licensing documents is a skip — the same IP discipline as the Halloween and Christmas guides.

Frequently asked questions

When do Valentine's Day stock lots hit the market?

Two windows: post-holiday residue clears late February–March (deepest discounts, but a 10–12 month hold), and early-buy supply appears November–January as factories and trading houses hold fresh production for the February peak (smaller discount, sell-through within the season). Missed-window cancelled containers appear sporadically at the deepest prices.

What do Easter decorations stock lots typically cost?

Indicative clearance ranges are roughly USD 0.05–0.4/pc for egg décor and kits, USD 0.5–2/pc for baskets and grass, USD 0.5–2.5/pc for bunny and Easter plush, USD 0.8–3/pc for garden flags and outdoor décor, and USD 0.05–0.5/pc for gift wrap and ribbons. Actual prices depend on quantity, condition and season.

Is buying Valentine or Easter stock counter-season worth it?

It depends on your hold capacity. Post-holiday residue is the deepest-priced and freshest-looking supply, but it carries a 10–12 month hold on a single-peak category — storage rent, tied-up capital and plush/paper aging partly offset the discount. In-season resellers often do better on early-buy lots priced just below wholesale.

What compliance checks apply to Valentine and Easter stock?

LED light-up props follow the battery shipping line (UN38.3 summaries, MSDS for lithium cells); egg-painting and craft kits follow children's-product toy-safety lines (EN71-style in the EU, ASTM F963-style in the US); scented candles and diffusers need SDS and hazard labelling; chocolate and filled confectionery are the no-go line — food cannot ship as ordinary clearance cargo.

What is the IP situation with Valentine and Easter designs?

This category is safer than Christmas or Halloween: generic hearts, cupids, teddy bears, bunnies and eggs are the bulk of supply and carry no licensing exposure. Skip lots with recognizable franchise or cartoon character prints unless licensing documents exist, and audit gift-box and ribbon prints for borrowed brand marks.
