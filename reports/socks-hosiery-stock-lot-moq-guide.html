Socks & Hosiery Stock Lots — MOQ, Pricing and QC Guide

How surplus socks, tights and underwear lots are quoted, packed and QC-checked in the China stock channel — MOQ logic when goods are already knitted, indicative per-dozen ranges, and a pre-payment checklist for importers, discount retailers and market traders.

Key takeaways.

  • MOQ logic flips for stock: new sock production runs to thousands of dozens per design; stock lots commonly start at one pallet or a few hundred cartons, with many listings showing MOQ from 100 pcs.
  • Indicative band: basic crew/ankle sock lots typically clear at about USD 2–12 per dozen depending on cotton content and grade; sports and compression styles run higher.
  • The size curve is the real price: surplus concentrates in popular sizes — demand the per-carton size breakdown in writing before comparing offers.
  • Mating pairs are the #1 defect in mixed or repacked hosiery lots; specify banded pairs and sample cartons on live video.
  • Supply concentrates in Zhejiang: the Zhuji Datang hosiery belt and Yiwu feed most export sock surplus; cancelled export orders and packaging resets are the main sources.

1. Where hosiery surplus comes from

Zhejiang province knits a disproportionate share of the world's socks: the Zhuji Datang belt alone concentrates tens of thousands of knitting machines, with Yiwu close by as the export and consolidation hub. Surplus forms through three recurring events:

  • Cancelled export orders. Knitted, paired, packed — then the buyer's LC expires or the shipment window is missed.
  • Over-runs and colour overshoot. Mills over-programme popular sizes and colours; the overshoot has no home channel.
  • Retail resets. Hypermarket chains rebrand packaging or drop suppliers; old-SKU hosiery must clear at any price.

Because socks are small, light and non-perishable, storage pressure is lower than for bulky goods — but assortment risk (sizes, colours, pairs) is higher. That trade-off defines how you should buy this category.

3. How MOQ actually works for sock stock

New-production MOQ is a machine-programming constraint: a mill needs thousands of dozens per design to justify a knitting run. Stock lots have no such constraint — the socks already exist — so the seller's MOQ reflects only packing and handling:

  • Typical stock MOQ: one pallet to a few hundred cartons; many listings on stock-clearance.ai show MOQ from 100 pcs or a single dozen-bundle.
  • Carton packing: commonly 100–300 pairs per carton, banded in dozens or poly-bagged; ask for pieces-per-carton and carton count before quoting freight.
  • Mixed lots: one carton may contain 3–10 styles. Mixed lots price lowest per dozen but demand the most QC — inspect on video, carton by carton.
  • Assortment buys: combining 2–3 sock lots with other light goods (underwear, hats, gloves) into one mixed container is standard Yiwu practice and typically improves landed cost per SKU by double digits.

4. The three risks unique to hosiery stock

Size curve

Surplus is a biased sample of production: over-runs concentrate in the sizes mills run most (for adult socks often EU 39–43). Get the size breakdown per carton in writing. If 70% of a lot is one size, your sell-through stalls regardless of how cheap the dozen looks.

Pairing

In mixed and repacked lots, single socks and mismatched pairs are the most common finding. Specify in writing: pairs mated and banded, no loose singles. Sample cartons on live video before payment.

Composition claims

"Cotton socks" may be 60% cotton or 25%. If your market labels composition (EU/US do), get fibre percentages per style and spot-check against labels in the carton. Mislabeled composition is a resale-compliance risk, not just a quality issue.

5. Pre-payment QC checklist

  1. Size breakdown per carton, in writing — then price the unpopular tail at a deep discount or as filler.
  2. Pairs mated and banded — no loose singles; verify on random-carton live video.
  3. Fibre composition per style — match hangtags/labels to declared percentages.
  4. Elastic and cuff check — old stock loses stretch; stretch-test samples older than 2 years.
  5. Individual sealing for underwear — factory-sealed packs protect resale value and hygiene perception.
  6. Third-party inspection for container-size orders — typically USD 100–200 per man-day in the Zhuji/Yiwu belt.

Frequently asked questions

How is MOQ different for sock stock lots?

New-production sock orders typically run to thousands of dozens per design because mills must programme knitting machines. Stock lots reverse this: the goods are already knitted and packed, so sellers commonly accept one pallet or a few hundred cartons, and many listings show MOQ from 100 pieces or a single dozen-bundle.

What do sock stock lots typically cost per dozen?

Indicative clearance ranges typically run from about USD 2 to 12 per dozen for basic crew and ankle socks depending on cotton content and grade, USD 4 to 15 per dozen for sports or compression styles, and roughly USD 0.5 to 3 per piece for tights and pantyhose. Ranges are indicative composites, not quotations.

What is the size-curve problem?

Surplus lots are rarely balanced across sizes — over-runs concentrate in popular sizes (for socks often EU 39–43). Ask for the size breakdown per carton in writing and price the unpopular tail at a deep discount or as filler, otherwise sell-through stalls.

Why must lots be checked for mating pairs?

In mixed or repacked lots, single socks and mismatched pairs are the most common defect. Specify in writing that pairs must be mated and banded, and sample cartons on live video before payment.

Where does Chinese hosiery surplus come from?

Mainly from the Zhuji Datang hosiery belt and Yiwu in Zhejiang, plus export-order cancellations and hypermarket packaging resets. When a season ends or an order is cancelled, finished socks are liquidated below cost to clear warehouse space.
