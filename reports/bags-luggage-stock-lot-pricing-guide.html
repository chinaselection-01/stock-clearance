Bags & Luggage Stock Lots — the B2B Pricing Guide

Where surplus backpacks, trolley cases and duffels come from, indicative price ranges by category, how to grade bag stock, and the hardware checks that decide whether a luggage lot is worth buying.

Key takeaways.

  • Bags and luggage are grading-sensitive stock: hardware, zippers and wheels — not fabric — decide whether a lot is A-grade or discounted B-grade.
  • Indicative clearance bands: totes typically USD 0.3–1.5/pc, backpacks USD 1.5–6/pc, duffels USD 2–6/pc, trolley sets USD 15–40/set.
  • Sources are structural: cancelled exports, seasonal colour obsolescence, corporate/airline merchandise oversupply and fair samples keep the pipeline full.
  • Branded OEM surplus discounts deepest — trademarks remain with their owners; lots are surplus, not authorised first-run retail goods.
  • Mixed lots are the norm: expect multi-colour, multi-SKU cartons; buy per lot, negotiate ex-warehouse, and mix categories into one container.

1. Why the bag & luggage pipeline is always full

Bags are a fashion-adjacent, hardware-heavy category — which makes them unusually prone to surplus. Four recurring generators:

  • Cancelled or unclaimed export orders. A finished container of branded backpacks that misses its sailing window moves into the secondary market within weeks.
  • Seasonal and colour obsolescence. Last year's colourway is unsellable at full price for the retailer but perfectly sellable in discount channels — the stock-lot market is that channel.
  • Corporate & airline merchandise oversupply. Promotional luggage and logo bags from events and airline programmes are made in bulk, then dumped at a fraction of cost.
  • Hardware and branding changes. A retailer switching zipper brands or logo placement strands the old production run in the factory warehouse.

The main supply belts are Guangzhou (fashion bags), Quanzhou (sports and backpacks), Pinghu (luggage cases) and Baigou (value-range bags). For importers this means below-cost buying is structurally normal in this category.

3. Grading bag stock: fabric is easy, hardware is not

A/B/mixed grading applies to bags as it does to apparel, but the inspection focus differs:

  • Grade A: clean cartons, consistent colour, zippers run full-length without snagging, hardware plating intact, straps and handles load-tested by hand.
  • Grade B: minor fabric marks, colour variation between cartons, plating dullness, or last-season branding — usually 20–40% below the A price for the same style.
  • Mixed lots: multi-SKU, multi-colour cartons priced per carton or per kilo. Standard for totes and pouches; acceptable for backpacks if the manifest lists styles.

For trolley cases specifically, ask the supplier to video three actions per sampled case: telescopic handle cycled 10 times, all four spinner wheels spun, and each zipper run end-to-end. These three checks catch the large majority of luggage stock defects before they become your customer returns.

4. Branded and licensed stock: the IP line

A recurring pattern in the belt: OEM surplus of branded luggage and bags — produced by the factory for a brand that cancelled, over-ordered, or changed direction. Discount retailers buy it because build quality and materials are first-run, while the price is secondary-market.

Trademark note: brands are mentioned for identification of goods in the secondary market. Trademarks belong to their owners; lots are surplus stock, not authorised first-run retail goods. Licensed-character goods (cartoon prints on kids' backpacks) carry additional IP risk — confirm your market's resale rules and avoid unlicensed character goods where enforcement is strict.

5. How to buy: a five-step checklist

Step 1 — Fix your freight math first

Luggage is the classic volume-eats-margin category: a 40ft container holds far fewer trolley sets than carton counts suggest. Ask for carton dimensions and packed volume before negotiating price.

Step 2 — Demand three proofs

  • date of production (adhesives and coatings age);
  • full carton photos including hardware close-ups;
  • a live warehouse video with a handwritten date card.

Step 3 — Sample-test the hardware

For pallet-plus orders, third-party inspection in Guangzhou/Quanzhou costs roughly USD 100–200 per man-day and typically pays for itself — zipper failures and missing hardware are the two most common findings in bag stock.

Step 4 — Negotiate on the lot

Stock sellers clear warehouses. "I take the whole lot, ex-warehouse, this week" gets a better price than per-piece haggling.

Step 5 — Mix your container

Bags pair well with apparel, footwear and household goods from 2–3 suppliers in one 40ft load — standard Yiwu consolidation, typically a double-digit improvement in landed cost per SKU.

Frequently asked questions

Where do bags and luggage stock lots come from?

Most surplus bag stock comes from cancelled export orders, seasonal colour obsolescence, airline and corporate merchandise oversupply, hardware or branding changes by retailers, and trade-fair samples. Guangzhou, Quanzhou, Pinghu and Baigou are the main supply belts.

What do luggage stock lots typically cost?

Indicative FOB-style clearance ranges: totes typically USD 0.3–1.5/pc, backpacks USD 1.5–6/pc, duffels USD 2–6/pc, school bags USD 1–4/pc, cosmetic pouches USD 0.2–0.8/pc, and 3-piece trolley sets USD 15–40/set. Ranges vary with quantity, material and brand.

What should I check on trolley-case stock before paying?

Test the telescopic handle through repeated extension cycles, spin each wheel, run every zipper full-length, and check lining stitching and shell cracks. Wheels, telescopic handles and zippers are the three most common failure points in luggage stock.

Can I resell branded bags or luggage from surplus lots?

Branded OEM surplus is factory-made for a brand that never took delivery or was over-supplied. Trademarks belong to their owners; lots are surplus stock, not authorised first-run retail goods. Confirm your destination market's resale rules before importing branded items.

What MOQ should I expect for bag stock?

Because the goods are already sewn and packed, many lots start from one pallet or a few hundred cartons. On stock-clearance.ai many bag and luggage listings show MOQ from 100 pieces, and mixed-colour lots are common.
