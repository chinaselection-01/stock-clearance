Shoes, Socks, Bags & Tableware Stock Lots: the B2B Buyer's Guide

How professional importers grade, price and QC four of the most-traded surplus categories from China — with MOQ benchmarks, container math and a pre-payment checklist.

Key takeaways.

  • All four categories share one rule: the goods are finished, so price is set by how fast the seller needs warehouse space — not by production cost.
  • Grading matters most in footwear: A-grade (new, boxed) vs B-grade (new, unboxed/mixed) can differ 40%+ in price and dictates your resale channel.
  • Socks are the easiest entry category: low value density, quoted per dozen/carton, ideal first container for new importers.
  • Tableware carries a food-contact compliance premium: export-order stock with test reports is worth paying extra for if you resell in the EU/US.
  • Mixed-category containers (shoes + socks + bags + tableware) are routine in Yiwu consolidation warehouses.

1. Footwear stock lots: learn the grading first

Shoes dominate the surplus channel because size runs break constantly. A retailer orders 10,000 pairs in a size curve; 8,000 sell; the remaining curve-tail becomes a lot. Buying vocabulary you need:

GradeWhat it meansTypical price vs new wholesaleResale channel
A / boxedBrand-new, original box, complete sizes50–80%Any retail/discount chain
B / unboxedNew shoes, no box or mixed boxes25–50%Markets, online flash, export to emerging markets
Mixed palletAssorted styles/sizes by carton15–40%Bin stores, per-kg or per-pair resellers

Always confirm: (1) are pairs matched in pairs — never accept " assorted" without pair-count confirmation; (2) size distribution in writing; (3) carton photos of actual stock, not catalogue renders.

2. Socks & hosiery: the highest-velocity surplus

Hosiery is the category where stock lots move fastest. Mills over-run constantly; a colour discontinued mid-run becomes 20,000 pairs of finished inventory overnight.

  • Quoting unit: per dozen pairs or per carton — never per pair for mixed lots.
  • Indicative stock pricing: basic cotton crew socks commonly clear below $0.3/pair in volume; children's and fashion socks range $0.1–0.5.
  • Check before paying: fibre content label language (EU/US-compliant?), needle count consistency, and whether elastic is aged (ask production date — old elastic fails the "stretch test" on arrival).

Because value density is low, socks often ride as filler cargo on top of heavier lots in a mixed container — a free margin layer if you negotiate freight right.

3. Bags & luggage: buy the hardware, not the fabric

Backpack, trolley and tote surplus fails on hardware more than fabric. Your QC focus in order of importance:

  1. Zippers — run every sample zipper 20 cycles; surplus zippers age badly in humid warehouses.
  2. Wheels & telescoping handles on trolleys — ask for a video of each sampled unit being rolled.
  3. Stitch density on handles — the load-bearing point; count stitches per inch on samples.
  4. SKU mix — single-SKU lots (all one backpack model) resell 2–3× faster than mixed lots at the same discount.

Indicative stock-lot ranges: backpacks $1.5–5/pc, trolley luggage $8–20/pc, tote/shopping bags $0.3–1.2/pc — heavily dependent on brand and hardware quality.

4. Tableware & kitchenware: pay for the paperwork

Cancelled export orders are the premium tier of tableware surplus because they arrive with food-contact documentation (EU 1935/2004, FDA CFR, LFGB on request). Domestic-market surplus is cheaper but often undocumented — a real problem if you resell into regulated markets.

  • Ask for: batch-tied test reports, glaze/lead test for ceramics, 304/316 marking verification for stainless.
  • Typical lots: dinner sets, kids' tableware (check for local toy-contact rules), stainless bowls and cutlery, melamine sets.
  • Indicative ranges: ceramic dinner sets $2–7/set, stainless cutlery sets $0.4–1.5/set, kids' melamine $0.3–1/pc.

Trademark note: where lots include branded goods (common in drinkware from OEM makers such as Haers, Tafuco, Fuguang, SHINETIME), trademarks belong to their owners; lots are secondary-market stock, not authorised first-run goods. Confirm your market's resale rules.

6. Pre-payment QC checklist (copy this)

  • ☐ Written grade definition (A/B/mixed) and pair/pc counts per carton
  • ☐ Production date for socks (elastic) and footwear (adhesives)
  • ☐ Food-contact reports batch-tied, for all tableware
  • ☐ Live warehouse video with handwritten date + lot code
  • ☐ Carton dimensions and gross weights → your forwarder's volume check
  • ☐ Third-party inspection booked before balance payment
  • ☐ Payment terms: 30% deposit / 70% after inspection is the market norm for stock

Frequently asked questions

Are shoes and socks stock lots new or used?

On stock-clearance.ai, lots are new finished goods: cancelled export orders, over-production and retailer channel resets. Used or returned goods exist in the wider market but must be explicitly labelled; if a listing doesn't state used/graded, ask before inquiring.

How is sock stock usually packed and priced?

Hosiery surplus is quoted per dozen pairs or per carton with mixed sizes and colours. Basic cotton socks commonly clear below $0.3 per pair in volume.

What should I check when buying bag and luggage lots?

Zipper function, wheel and telescoping-handle operation, handle stitch density, and SKU mix. Single-SKU lots resell 2–3× faster than mixed lots at the same discount.

Is tableware stock food-safe for EU or US resale?

Ask for batch-tied food-contact test reports (EU 1935/2004 or FDA CFR). Cancelled export orders almost always have them; domestic surplus often does not — which is why export-order stock carries a premium.

What MOQ should I expect?

Footwear and socks commonly start at 500–1000 pairs per style or by carton; bags at 200–500 pcs; tableware by carton or pallet. Single-pallet mixed lots are realistic entry points.
