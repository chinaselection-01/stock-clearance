China Toy Closeout & Overstock Stock Lots — MOQ & Grading Guide

Where surplus toys come from, what they typically clear for, how A/B/mixed grading works, and the safety-compliance checks every importer of toy closeout stock should run before payment.

Key takeaways.

  • Toys are the most seasonal surplus category: closeout volume peaks after Christmas, Children's Day and retailer assortment resets.
  • Indicative pricing: toy stock lots typically clear in a USD 0.2–3.0/pc band — plush and simple plastic at the low end, battery-operated and ride-on toys at the high end.
  • MOQ flips low: goods are finished, so lots often start from one pallet or from 100 pcs on stock-clearance.ai.
  • Grading matters more than in most categories: A-grade is export-standard brand-new stock; B-grade and mixed lots price deepest but carry missing-parts and packaging risk.
  • Compliance does not get cheaper with the goods: EN71 (EU) and ASTM F963/CPSIA (US) apply to stock toys exactly as to new production.

1. Why toy closeout keeps flooding the market

China's toy belts — Shantou (Chenghai) for plastic toys, Yangzhou for plush, Ningbo and Yiwu for mixed general toys — produce far ahead of retail demand. Four recurring events create surplus:

  • Seasonal overshoot. Factories ramp for Christmas and Children's Day; whatever misses the shipping window becomes a January or June liquidation problem.
  • Cancelled export orders. A finished container of toys that misses its sailing date is liquidated below cost — the factory needs the space, not the inventory.
  • Character-licence expiry. Licensed character toys (cartoon and movie IP) must be cleared when the licence lapses; this produces some of the deepest discounts in the category, but resale restrictions can apply in some markets.
  • Retailer resets. When a chain drops a supplier or rebrands packaging, the old SKU must move at any price.

3. Grading: A, B and mixed lots

Toy grading is stricter than household goods because packaging is part of the product's shelf appeal:

  • A-grade: brand-new, export-standard packaging, full sets. Expect the top of the price band — typically 30–60% below comparable new-production wholesale.
  • B-grade: shop-soiled cartons, dented packaging, or discontinued artwork. Goods are usually functional; the discount is typically 50–75%.
  • Mixed lots: assorted SKUs in assorted condition, often sold per carton or per kg. Deepest discounts, but require per-carton inspection and a written set-completeness declaration.

Practical rule: for plush, mixed lots are usually safe value; for set-based plastic toys, pay for A-grade or inspect every carton.

4. Safety compliance: stock is not exempt

  • EU: EN71 parts 1–3 apply to toys regardless of price point. Stock produced for the EU market normally has EN71 documentation behind it — ask for the test report copy.
  • US: ASTM F963 and CPSIA labelling (tracking labels, lead limits) apply. Stock made for other markets is often not US-compliant.
  • Battery toys: EU battery regulations and WEEE obligations follow the importer; check what battery type is included and whether the destination allows it.
  • Licensed characters: licence expiry may restrict resale in some territories. Confirm the licence status in writing before importing branded character toys.

5. How to buy: a five-step checklist

Step 1 — Match the stock to your market first

Ask one question before price: "Which market was this certified for?" EU-certified stock into the US (or vice versa) is the most expensive mistake in toy clearance buying.

Step 2 — Demand three proofs

  • date of manufacture and certification report where available;
  • full carton photos including inner trays;
  • a live warehouse video with a handwritten date card.

Step 3 — Inspect per carton for mixed lots

Third-party inspection in Shantou/Yiwu typically runs USD 100–200 per man-day. Missing pieces and mixed grades are the two findings that most often justify the fee.

Step 4 — Get set-completeness in writing

Require the seller to declare set completeness and battery status on the proforma invoice — it converts a verbal promise into a claimable term.

Step 5 — Mix your container

Consolidate toys + stationery + household goods from 2–3 suppliers into one 40ft load. Party-favour toys are dense and cheap per carton, which makes them ideal container fillers.

Frequently asked questions

Why do toy closeout lots flood the market after peak seasons?

Toys are produced ahead of retail seasons. After Christmas, Children's Day or a retailer's assortment reset, finished toys that never shipped become surplus and are liquidated to free warehouse space before the next production cycle.

What does toy overstock typically cost per piece?

Indicatively, toy stock lots commonly clear in a USD 0.2–3.0 per piece band: plush and simple plastic toys at the low end, battery-operated and ride-on toys at the high end. Licensed character toys vary most, depending on licence status.

What MOQ should I expect?

Because closeout toys are already produced, MOQ is typically one pallet or a few hundred cartons rather than a production run. Many listings on stock-clearance.ai start from 100 pieces or a single pallet.

Do toy safety rules still apply to clearance stock?

Yes. EN71 applies in the EU and ASTM F963/CPSIA in the US regardless of price. Ask which market the stock was certified for, request the test report where it exists, and avoid importing uncertified battery-operated toys into regulated markets.

What is the biggest QC risk in mixed toy lots?

Set-completeness. Multi-part sets often have missing pieces in mixed-grade lots. Inspect per carton and require the seller to declare set completeness in writing before payment.
